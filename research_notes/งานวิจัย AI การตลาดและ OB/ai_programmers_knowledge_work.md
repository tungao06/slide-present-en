## Key Question 1: Which studies of AI and developer/knowledge-worker productivity (2021–2026) are most credible and most cited, and which have been published in peer-reviewed journals versus remaining working papers?

### Takeaway
The core canon is now mostly peer-reviewed: Brynjolfsson–Li–Raymond (QJE 2025), Noy–Zhang (Science 2023), Cui et al. (Management Science 2025), Dell'Acqua et al. jagged frontier (Organization Science 2026) and the Cybernetic Teammate (Organization Science online). The two most-quoted developer studies, Peng et al. (2023, +55.8%) and the METR 2025 RCT (−19%), remain arXiv working papers, which matters for an MBA report because they have not passed journal review.

### Cited Findings

**Paper 1 — Peng, Kalliamvakou, Cihon & Demirer (2023), "The Impact of AI on Developer Productivity: Evidence from GitHub Copilot"**
- arXiv:2302.06590, submitted 13 Feb 2023; authors at Microsoft Research, GitHub Inc. and MIT Sloan; free PDF on arXiv; also listed on Microsoft Research — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2302.06590); [Microsoft Research](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/research/publication/the-impact-of-ai-on-developer-productivity-evidence-from-github-copilot)
- Design: controlled experiment; recruited developers implemented an HTTP server in JavaScript as fast as possible; treatment group with Copilot finished 55.8% faster than control; authors note heterogeneous effects suggesting help for people transitioning into software careers — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2302.06590)
- Status: working paper only; searches surfaced no journal version. Secondary sources repeat "95 freelance developers, 71 vs 161 minutes," but those numbers were not visible in the abstract retrieved and should be checked against the PDF — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2302.06590v1)
- Summary for MBA use: A single short task, a small freelancer sample and a GitHub/Microsoft author team make this the most-quoted and most-criticised number in the field; the headline 55.8% is a good anchor for a class discussion on what "productivity" means and on who funds the research. Difficulty: easy. Presentation angle: open with "the number your CTO quotes" and then show how later, larger studies (Cui et al., METR) revised it. Free: yes (arXiv).

**Paper 2 — Cui, Demirer, Jaffe, Musolff, Peng & Salz, "The Effects of Generative AI on High-Skilled Work: Evidence from Three Field Experiments with Software Developers"**
- Published in Management Science, DOI 10.1287/mnsc.2025.00535 (INFORMS page lists it; accepted by Jean-Pierre Dubé, marketing department); SSRN preprint 4945566 posted Sept 2024, last revised Aug 2025; also on Microsoft Research — [INFORMS](https://pubsonline.informs.org/doi/fpi/10.1287/mnsc.2025.00535); [SSRN](https://papers.ssrn.com/abstract=4945566); [Microsoft Research](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/research/?p=1148213)
- Design: RCTs at Microsoft, Accenture and an anonymous Fortune 100 firm, run by the companies in the ordinary course of business, randomly giving developers GitHub Copilot; pooled 4,867 developers; 26.08% increase in completed tasks (SE 10.3%); less-experienced developers had higher adoption and larger gains; authors stress individual experiments are noisy and vary — [SSRN](https://papers.ssrn.com/abstract=4945566); [INFORMS](https://pubsonline.informs.org/doi/fpi/10.1287/mnsc.2025.00535)
- Pre-registered in the AEA RCT Registry (AEARCTR-0014530), intervention GitHub Copilot — [AEA Registry](https://www.socialscienceregistry.org/trials/14530)
- Summary for MBA use: The largest real-workplace evidence on AI coding tools; the ~26% gain in pull requests/tasks comes with wide uncertainty and a clear tenure gradient (juniors adopt more and gain more). Managerial implications: rollout design, who to train first, and why pooled multi-firm evidence beats a single pilot. Difficulty: medium (econometrics in body, but the intro is readable). Presentation angle: "Microsoft, Accenture and a Fortune 100 ran the experiment for us." Free: yes (SSRN preprint; journal version paywalled).

**Paper 3 — Dell'Acqua, McFowland III, Mollick, Lifshitz-Assaf, Kellogg, Rajendran, Krayer, Candelon & Lakhani, "Navigating the Jagged Technological Frontier: Field Experimental Evidence of the Effects of Artificial Intelligence on Knowledge Worker Productivity and Quality"**
- SSRN 4573321, posted 18 Sept 2023, last revised 17 Mar 2026; HBS Technology & Operations Mgt. Unit Working Paper 24-013; now published in Organization Science (INFORMS) 2026, vol. 37, no. 2, as lead article; DOI reported as 10.1287/orsc.2025.21838 — [SSRN](https://papers.ssrn.com/abstract=4573321); [EBSCO record](https://www.ebsco.com/articles/social-sciences-and-humanities/ae8300a6-197d-54c5-b89b-a9c0240104cb/navigating-the-jagged-technological-frontier-field-experimental-evidence-of-the-effects-of-artificial-intelligence-on-knowledge-worker-productivity-and-quality); [AOM OB division announcement of the March–April 2026 issue](https://connect.aom.org/discussion/new-issue-of-organization-science-5)
- Design and headline numbers: preregistered experiment with 758 BCG consultants randomly assigned to no AI, GPT-4, or GPT-4 plus a prompt-engineering overview; inside the frontier, AI users completed 12.2% more tasks, 25.1% faster, with quality rated more than 40% higher; outside the frontier, AI use reduced performance — [SSRN](https://papers.ssrn.com/abstract=4573321); [MIT Sloan mirror of the WP PDF](https://mitsloan.mit.edu/sites/default/files/2023-10/SSRN-id4573321.pdf)
- Organization Science's editors describe it as "one of the most influential pieces on AI and work in recent years" — [AOM announcement](https://connect.aom.org/discussion/new-issue-of-organization-science-5)
- Author Ethan Mollick's accessible write-up introduces the "centaurs and cyborgs" framing — [One Useful Thing](https://www.oneusefulthing.org/p/centaurs-and-cyborgs-on-the-jagged)
- Summary for MBA use: The go-to OB paper on generative AI: large gains inside the "frontier," losses outside it (consultants were about 19 percentage points less likely to be right on a task designed to fool the model, per the WP abstract — verify in PDF), and bottom-half performers gained most. Managerial implications: the manager's job becomes mapping the frontier and training judgement about when to trust AI. Difficulty: easy–medium; the introduction is self-contained and widely taught. Presentation angle: the jagged-frontier picture and the centaur/cyborg metaphor are very presentable. Free: yes (SSRN/HBS working paper; journal version paywalled). Caveat: DOI 10.1287/orsc.2025.21838 came from an editor's announcement, not the INFORMS page itself.

**Paper 4 — Brynjolfsson, Li & Raymond, "Generative AI at Work"**
- Quarterly Journal of Economics 140(2), 2025, pp. 889–942, DOI 10.1093/qje/qjae044; first circulated as NBER WP 31161 (April 2023) and arXiv:2304.11771 — [SUFE record](https://academicnewsletter.sufe.edu.cn/info/344659); [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2304.11771); [Raymond CV listing](https://economics.mit.edu/sites/default/files/2026-07/Raymond_CV.pdf)
- Page-range/issue discrepancy: Raymond's CV lists pp. 889–944, vol. 140 issue 3 (May 2025) vs. other sources 889–942, issue 2 — check the OUP page before citing — [Raymond CV](https://economics.mit.edu/sites/default/files/2026-07/Raymond_CV.pdf); [Passau bibliography](https://www.ibusiness.uni-passau.de/fileadmin/dokumente/fakultaeten/wiwi/lehrstuehle/kraemer/Abschlussarbeiten/WiSE25_GenAI_Work.pdf)
- Findings (published version): staggered rollout of a GenAI conversational assistant to 5,172 customer-support agents; productivity (issues resolved per hour) up 15% on average with large heterogeneity; the working paper reported 14% average and a 34% gain for novice/low-skilled workers with minimal effect on experienced workers — [SUFE record](https://academicnewsletter.sufe.edu.cn/info/344659); [arXiv v1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2304.11771v1); [BLS Monthly Labor Review summary](https://www.bls.gov/opub/mlr/2024/beyond-bls/ai-improved-the-productivity-of-a-fortune-500-software-company.htm)
- Summary for MBA use: The flagship peer-reviewed field study of GenAI at work: AI disseminates top performers' tacit knowledge to novices, compresses the experience curve, and (in the WP) improved customer sentiment and reduced attrition and requests for managerial help. Managerial implications: onboarding, retention of junior staff, and the risk of hollowing out the learning ladder. Difficulty: medium–hard (QJE econometrics), but the introduction is readable. Presentation angle: "a 2-month novice with AI performs like a 6-month veteran." Free: yes (NBER WP / arXiv; QJE version paywalled).

**Paper 5 — Noy & Zhang, "Experimental evidence on the productivity effects of generative artificial intelligence"**
- Science, 14 July 2023, pp. 187–192, DOI 10.1126/science.adh2586 — [SUFE record](https://academicnewsletter.sufe.edu.cn/info/256290); [Stanford SCALE listing](https://scale.stanford.edu/publications/experimental-evidence-productivity-effects-generative-artificial-intelligence); [MIT working paper PDF](https://economics.mit.edu/sites/default/files/inline-files/Noy_Zhang_1_0.pdf)
- Findings: preregistered online experiment, 453 college-educated professionals doing incentivized occupation-specific writing tasks, half given ChatGPT; average task time fell 40%, quality rose 18%; inequality between workers fell; exposed participants were about twice as likely to be using ChatGPT at their real job two weeks later and 1.6x at two months. The March 2023 WP reported n=444 and effects of 0.8 SD (time) and 0.4 SD (quality) — [SUFE record](https://academicnewsletter.sufe.edu.cn/info/256290); [Marginal Revolution summary](https://marginalrevolution.com/marginalrevolution/2023/07/shakked-noy-and-whitney-zhang-on-the-productivity-effects-of-llms.html)
- Summary for MBA use: Short, clean Science paper; the mechanism was substitution (workers let ChatGPT draft and edited less) rather than complementing effort, and lower-ability writers gained most, compressing inequality. Managerial implications: AI may flatten skill differences in writing-heavy roles and change what "quality" is rewarded. Difficulty: easy. Presentation angle: the quality/time scatter and the "did people keep using it?" follow-up. Free: yes (MIT WP PDF; Science paywalled).

**Paper 6 — Becker, Rush, Barnes & Rein (METR), "Measuring the Impact of Early-2025 AI on Experienced Open-Source Developer Productivity"**
- arXiv:2507.09089, submitted 12 July 2025, v2 25 July 2025; METR blog 10 July 2025; working paper (no journal version found) — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.09089v1); [METR blog](https://metr.org/blog/2025-07-10-early-2025-ai-experienced-os-dev-study/)
- Design and findings: RCT with 16 experienced open-source developers, 246 tasks on mature repositories they knew (avg. 5 years' experience); each task randomized to allow/disallow AI (mainly Cursor Pro with Claude 3.5/3.7 Sonnet); developers forecast AI would cut time by 24%, afterwards believed it had cut time by 20%, but AI actually increased completion time by 19%; economics experts had predicted 39% and ML experts 38% speedups — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.09089v1)
- Authors warn against over-generalising: high repository familiarity and repo maturity contribute to the slowdown and do not apply in many settings — [METR blog](https://metr.org/blog/2025-07-10-early-2025-ai-experienced-os-dev-study/)
- Summary for MBA use: The most surprising developer study: senior engineers were slower with AI yet believed they were faster, a 39-point perception gap. Managerial implications: self-reported productivity and acceptance-rate dashboards can mislead; context (codebase maturity, tacit knowledge) determines returns; measurement must be experimental. Difficulty: easy (readable, lots of figures). Presentation angle: the forecast-vs-actual bar chart is a one-slide show-stopper. Free: yes (arXiv). Caveat: not peer-reviewed; n=16.

**Paper 7 — Dell'Acqua, Ayoubi, Lifshitz, Sadun, E. Mollick, L. Mollick, Han, Goldman, Nair, Taub & Lakhani, "The Cybernetic Teammate: A Field Experiment on Generative AI Reshaping Teamwork and Expertise"**
- NBER Working Paper 33641 (April 2025), DOI 10.3386/w33641; SSRN 5188231 (posted 21 Mar 2025); a version is published online in Organization Science under the shortened title "The Cybernetic Teammate: A Field Experiment on Generative AI and Teamwork," DOI 10.1287/orsc.2025.20702; the arXiv ID 2503.15351 could not be confirmed by search — [NBER](https://www.nber.org/papers/w33641); [SSRN](https://papers.ssrn.com/abstract=5188231); [INFORMS](https://pubsonline.informs.org/doi/fpi/10.1287/orsc.2025.20702)
- Findings: 776 Procter & Gamble professionals randomly assigned to work with/without AI and individually/in pairs on new-product development; individuals with AI matched the performance of two-person teams without AI; without AI, R&D staff proposed technical and Commercial staff proposed commercial solutions, while with AI both produced balanced solutions regardless of background — [NBER](https://www.nber.org/papers/w33641)
- Summary for MBA use: Directly about team design: AI acted as a teammate that substituted for a second person and dissolved functional silos; the WP also reports more positive emotions among AI users (verify in PDF). Managerial implications: team size, cross-functional staffing and the social role of AI. Difficulty: medium. Presentation angle: "one person plus AI equals a two-person team." Free: yes (NBER/SSRN).

**Paper 8 — Lee, Sarkar, Tankelevitch, Drosos, Rintel, Banks & Wilson (Microsoft Research), "The Impact of Generative AI on Critical Thinking: Self-Reported Reductions in Cognitive Effort and Confidence Effects From a Survey of Knowledge Workers"**
- CHI 2025 proceedings, Article 1121, pp. 1–22, DOI 10.1145/3706598.3713778 (peer-reviewed conference) — [Microsoft Research](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/research/?p=1135061); [author PDF](https://advait.org/files/lee_2025_ai_critical_thinking_survey.pdf)
- Findings: survey of 319 knowledge workers sharing 936 first-hand GenAI use examples; higher confidence in GenAI predicted less critical thinking, higher self-confidence predicted more; critical thinking shifts toward verification, integration and "task stewardship"; workers report less effort on routine low-stakes tasks under time pressure — [Microsoft Research](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/research/?p=1135061); [Microsoft Research CHI 2025 roundup](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/research/?p=1136647)
- Summary for MBA use: The leading evidence base for the "cognitive offloading/deskilling" worry, with a clear managerial lever (calibrated confidence, not blanket trust). Difficulty: easy (survey, descriptive). Presentation angle: pairs well with METR's perception gap. Free: yes. Caveat: self-report, not measured cognition.

**Paper 9 — Hoffmann, Boysel, Nagle, Peng & Xu, "Generative AI and the Nature of Work"**
- Harvard Business School Working Paper 25-021; earlier NBER conference version (July 2024) titled "Generative AI and Distributed Work: Evidence from Open Source Software"; Wharton-hosted draft dated 27 Oct 2024; working paper, not peer-reviewed — [Wharton Mack Institute PDF](https://mackinstitute.wharton.upenn.edu/wp-content/uploads/2025/04/WTIC.2025_Nagle-Frank_Generative-AI.pdf); [NBER conference PDF](https://conference.nber.org/conf_papers/f207681.pdf); [Marginal Revolution](https://marginalrevolution.com/marginalrevolution/2024/11/generative-ai-and-the-nature-of-work.html)
- Findings: regression-discontinuity design around GitHub's free-Copilot eligibility threshold for top open-source maintainers, millions of activities over two years, ~187,000 developers; Copilot access shifted time toward core coding and away from project-management work, increased autonomous over collaborative work and exploration over exploitation; effects larger for lower-ability developers — [MIT Sloan Ideas Made to Matter](https://mitsloan.mit.edu/node/51270); [AI Governance Library summary](https://www.aigl.blog/generative-ai-and-the-nature-of-work/)
- Summary for MBA use: The best evidence that AI changes the content of a developer's job, not just speed — less coordination, more solo exploration. Managerial implications: coordination costs, team cohesion and the hidden loss of collaborative learning. Difficulty: medium–hard (RDD). Free: yes.

**Paper 10 — Paradis, Grey, Madison, Nam, Macvean, Meimand, Zhang, Ferrari-Church & Chandra (Google), "How much does AI impact development speed? An enterprise-based randomized controlled trial"**
- arXiv:2410.12944 (v1–v3); presented in the ICSE 2025 Software Engineering in Practice track, Ottawa, 2 May 2025 (peer-reviewed) — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.12944v3); [ICSE 2025 SEIP program](https://conf.researchr.org/details/icse-2025/icse-2025-software-engineering-in-practice/26/How-much-does-AI-impact-development-speed-An-enterprise-based-randomized-controlled-)
- Findings: 96 full-time Google engineers; complex enterprise-grade task (per a secondary summary, editing 10 files/474 lines to build a logging service; ~96 min with AI vs 114 without); central estimate ~21% time reduction after controls, wide CI; developers who spend more hours/day coding gained more; authors caution results reflect Google's internal tooling as of summer 2024 — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.12944v2); [GetDX newsletter](https://newsletter.getdx.com/p/how-much-does-ai-impact-development-speed)
- Summary for MBA use: A realistic middle estimate between Peng's 55.8% and METR's −19%. Difficulty: easy. Free: yes.

### Inferences
- The field has converged from one-off lab tasks (Peng 2023) to multi-firm pre-registered RCTs (Cui 2025) and journal publication; a 2026 MBA report should lead with the journal versions and cite working papers (METR, Hoffmann) as "working paper, not peer-reviewed."
- Effect sizes shrink as settings get more realistic: +55.8% (toy task) → +26% (enterprise rollout, noisy) → +21% (Google, complex task) → −19% (experts in mature codebases). This ordering is itself a presentable storyline about measurement and context.
- Industry-authored studies (GitHub/Microsoft/Google) dominate the developer-specific evidence; independent replications (METR, academic open-source studies) are the natural counterweight and worth flagging for conflict-of-interest discussion.

## Key Question 2: Which studies have surprising findings (juniors gaining most, experienced developers slower, perceived-vs-actual gaps, falling cognitive effort or skill atrophy)?

### Takeaway
Four robust "surprises" recur: (1) low-tenure and lower-ability workers gain the most (Brynjolfsson et al., Cui et al., Noy & Zhang, Dell'Acqua et al., Hoffmann et al.); (2) experienced developers in familiar, mature codebases got 19% slower while believing they were 20% faster (METR); (3) AI used as a crutch harms learning and security while raising confidence (Bastani et al., Perry et al., Lee et al.); and (4) human–AI combinations often underperform the better of the two alone, especially on decision tasks (Vaccaro et al.).

### Cited Findings
- Novices/low-skill gain most: 34% productivity gain for novice and low-skilled agents vs minimal effect for experienced, highly skilled agents (working-paper version) — [arXiv 2304.11771v1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2304.11771v1); less-experienced developers had higher adoption and greater productivity gains across three firms — [SSRN 4945566](https://papers.ssrn.com/abstract=4945566); inequality between writers decreased because lower-ability workers benefited more — [Marginal Revolution](https://marginalrevolution.com/marginalrevolution/2023/07/shakked-noy-and-whitney-zhang-on-the-productivity-effects-of-llms.html); main effects of Copilot access larger for relatively lower-ability developers — [AI Governance Library](https://www.aigl.blog/generative-ai-and-the-nature-of-work/)
- Experts slower, perception inverted: forecast −24%, post-hoc belief −20%, actual +19% completion time; expert economists predicted −39% and ML experts −38% — [arXiv 2507.09089](https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.09089v1)
- Perceived productivity tracks acceptance rate rather than code that actually survives (GitHub's own data) — [arXiv 2205.06537](https://arxiv.org/abs/2205.06537v1)
- Outside the frontier, AI lowers accuracy: consultants using GPT-4 on a task outside the model's capability performed worse than controls — [SSRN 4573321](https://papers.ssrn.com/abstract=4573321)
- Skill atrophy: students with unrestricted GPT-4 scored 48% higher on practice problems but 17% lower on the unaided exam than students who never had access; a guardrailed tutor (+127% practice) largely eliminated the exam penalty; students used GPT-4 "as a crutch" — Bastani, Bastani, Sungu, Ge, Kabakcı & Mariman, PNAS 122(26), 1 July 2025, DOI 10.1073/pnas.2422633122 — [IDEAS record](https://ideas.repec.org/a/nas/journl/v122y2025pe2422633122.html); [Knowledge at Wharton](https://knowledge.wharton.upenn.edu/article/without-guardrails-generative-ai-can-harm-education/); [SSRN preprint](https://papers.ssrn.com/abstract=4895486)
- Overconfidence in security: Perry, Srivastava, Kumar & Boneh, "Do Users Write More Insecure Code with AI Assistants?", ACM CCS 2023, DOI 10.1145/3576915.3623157, found participants with an AI assistant wrote less secure code yet were more likely to believe their code was secure — [arXiv 2211.03622](https://arxiv.org/abs/2211.03622v1); [GetDX summary](https://getdx.com/research/do-developers-write-insecure-code-with-ai-assistants/)
- Reduced critical thinking: higher confidence in GenAI associated with less critical thinking among 319 knowledge workers — [Microsoft Research](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/research/?p=1135061)
- Widening gap for novices: Prather et al., "The Widening Gap: The Benefits and Harms of Generative AI for Novice Programmers," ICER 2024 (Melbourne, Aug 2024), arXiv:2405.17739; GenAI "can provide complete solutions to most introductory programming problems," and the paper examines the metacognitive harms for students who struggle — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.17739v1); [ICER 2024 program](https://icer2024.acm.org/details/icer-2024-papers/26/The-Widening-Gap-The-Benefits-and-Harms-of-Generative-AI-for-Novice-Programmers)
- Human–AI teams often worse than the best solo performer: Hedges' g = −0.23 (95% CI −0.39 to −0.07); losses in decision tasks, gains in content creation; gains when the human alone beats the AI, losses when AI alone is stronger — [PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11659167/)
- Delivery metrics can fall even as code quality rises: −7.2% stability and −1.5% throughput per 25% AI adoption increase (industry survey, not peer-reviewed) — [DORA infographic](https://dora.dev/research/2024/2024-DORA-Report-Infographic.pdf)
- Entry-level exposure: 22–25-year-olds in AI-exposed occupations down 16% relative (ADP payroll, working paper) — [Stanford DEL](https://digitaleconomy.stanford.edu/publications/canaries-in-the-coal-mine)

### Inferences
- The "juniors gain most" pattern and the "entry-level hiring falls" pattern point in opposite directions for a programmer's career strategy, which makes a strong discussion question for OB (skill formation vs. labor-demand effects).
- METR's inverted perception plus Ziegler's acceptance-rate finding suggest that engineering dashboards built on self-report or suggestion acceptance will systematically overstate AI ROI.

### Gaps
- Exact participant counts and effect sizes for Perry et al. (CCS 2023) and Prather et al. (ICER 2024) were not visible in the search snippets; the Prather ACM DOI (believed to be 10.1145/3632620.3671116) could not be confirmed — use the arXiv link.
- No peer-reviewed study of long-run skill atrophy among professional developers (as opposed to students) was found; the deskilling evidence for programmers is indirect (students, security, self-report).

## Key Question 4: Which papers connect to OB themes a programmer-turned-MBA could discuss (motivation, skill development, inequality within teams, managing hybrid human–AI teams)?

### Takeaway
Each OB theme has a strong anchor paper: skill development and inequality within teams (Brynjolfsson et al.; Cui et al.; Bastani et al.), trust and over-reliance (METR; Perry et al.; Lee et al.; jagged frontier), task design and autonomy (Hoffmann et al.), and hybrid human–AI team design (Cybernetic Teammate; Vaccaro et al. meta-analysis).

### Cited Findings
- Knowledge diffusion and the experience curve: AI assistance let novices approach experienced-agent performance, raising productivity 14–15% on average and up to 34% for novices — [arXiv 2304.11771v1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2304.11771v1); [SUFE QJE record](https://academicnewsletter.sufe.edu.cn/info/344659)
- Within-team inequality: lower-ability developers and bottom-half consultants gain most, compressing performance dispersion — [AI Governance Library on Hoffmann et al.](https://www.aigl.blog/generative-ai-and-the-nature-of-work/); [SSRN 4573321](https://papers.ssrn.com/abstract=4573321)
- Autonomy vs collaboration: Copilot access shifted developers toward autonomous work and exploration, away from collaborative project management — [MIT Sloan](https://mitsloan.mit.edu/node/51270)
- Team composition: individuals with AI matched two-person teams without AI; AI erased the R&D-vs-Commercial bias in proposals — [NBER w33641](https://www.nber.org/papers/w33641)
- Trust calibration: confidence in GenAI predicts less critical thinking; self-confidence predicts more — [Microsoft Research](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/research/?p=1135061); developers' beliefs about speedup were 39 points off reality — [arXiv 2507.09089](https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.09089v1)
- Learning design: guardrails (GPT Tutor) preserved learning while unguarded access harmed it (−17% on exams) — [Knowledge at Wharton](https://knowledge.wharton.upenn.edu/article/without-guardrails-generative-ai-can-harm-education/)
- When to combine humans and AI: combinations help on creative/content tasks and hurt on decision tasks; the better solo performer should lead — [PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11659167/)
- Firm-level rollout and adoption: the three-firm RCT found adoption itself varied with tenure, affecting who realises gains — [SSRN 4945566](https://papers.ssrn.com/abstract=4945566)
- Labor-market framing for careers: entry-level AI-exposed employment down 16% relative (working paper) vs. precise nulls on earnings/hours in Denmark (working paper) — [Stanford DEL](https://digitaleconomy.stanford.edu/publications/canaries-in-the-coal-mine); [IDEAS/NBER 33777](https://ideas.repec.org/p/nbr/nberwo/33777.html)
- Follow-on adoption as a motivation outcome: participants exposed once to ChatGPT were twice as likely to be using it at work two weeks later — [SUFE Science record](https://academicnewsletter.sufe.edu.cn/info/256290)

### Inferences
- Suggested presentation arc for the student: (1) the hype number (Peng 55.8%); (2) the realistic enterprise estimate (Cui 26%, Paradis 21%); (3) the shock (METR −19% with inverted perception); (4) the OB mechanisms (junior uplift, skill atrophy, trust calibration, team redesign); (5) managerial playbook (map the frontier, measure experimentally, protect learning ladders, redesign teams).
- Managerial implications common to the credible studies: deploy first where juniors do routine, in-frontier work; build verification habits rather than blanket trust; measure outcomes (merged PRs, defects, stability) rather than acceptance rates or self-report; and protect deliberate practice for junior engineers given both the learning-crutch evidence and the entry-level hiring decline.

### Gaps
- No peer-reviewed study was found that directly measures intrinsic motivation or job satisfaction of professional developers using AI tools with an experimental design; GitHub's survey-based satisfaction claims are vendor research, and METR/Cui focus on output.
- Evidence on team dynamics in software firms specifically (as opposed to P&G product teams or open-source maintainers) is limited to Hoffmann et al.'s observational open-source data.
