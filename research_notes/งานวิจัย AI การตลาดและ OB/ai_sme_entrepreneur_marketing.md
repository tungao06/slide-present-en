## Key Question 1: Which 2021–2026 papers on AI/GenAI and SME or entrepreneur marketing are most cited and freely accessible? (Candidate shortlist, 12 papers + 3 clearly labelled working papers)

### Takeaway
The strongest classroom candidates are (1) Otis et al. (Management Science, 2026; free eScholarship PDF) – a 640-firm randomized field experiment giving Kenyan micro-entrepreneurs a GPT-4 business adviser, (2) Hartmann, Exner & Domdey (IJRM, 2025) on whether AI images beat human marketing images in real Meta ad campaigns, (3) Reisenbichler et al. (Marketing Science, 2022) on machine-written SEO content beating human experts, and (4) Shepherd & Majchrzak (JBV, 2022), the most-cited conceptual paper on AI augmenting entrepreneurs (135–207 Scopus citations). Fully open-access options exist in every category: Badghish & Soomro (Sustainability, 2024, TOE/SMEs), Chummee (Asia Social Issues, 2026, Thai SMEs/TOE), Upadhyay et al. (IJEBR, 2022, CC-BY-NC manuscript), Gastmann & Bastos (JMC, 2025, repository PDF), and García Rellán et al. (Redmarka, 2025, food-service Instagram campaign built with ChatGPT).

### Cited Findings

#### Paper 1 — Otis, Clarke, Delecourt, Holtz & Koning — "The Uneven Impact of Generative AI on Entrepreneurial Performance: Evidence from a Field Experiment in Kenya"
- Authors: Nicholas Otis, Rowan Clarke, Solène Delecourt, David Holtz, Rembrand Koning. First circulated as Harvard Business School Working Paper 24-042 (2023); later version listed in Management Science with publication date 2026-07-10 and DOI 10.1287/mnsc.2024.06909 — [Berkeley Haas newsroom](https://newsroom.haas.berkeley.edu/research/gen-ai-experiment-shows-mixed-results-in-helping-small-businesses-grow/); [HKS CID listing](https://www.hks.harvard.edu/centers/cid/publications/uneven-impact-generative-ai-entrepreneurial-performance); [INFORMS DOI page](https://pubsonline.informs.org/doi/10.1287/mnsc.2024.06909)
- Volume/issue: not yet visible in search results (appears as an "articles in advance"/online-first item); DOI seen verbatim on the INFORMS URL — [pubsonline.informs.org](https://pubsonline.informs.org/doi/10.1287/mnsc.2024.06909)
- Open access: free full text of the Management Science version via University of California eScholarship — [eScholarship PDF](https://escholarship.org/content/qt0fb001r8/qt0fb001r8.pdf); also the NBER conference PDF — [NBER](https://conference.nber.org/conf_papers/f194654.pdf)
- Method: randomized controlled trial, May–November 2023, 640 Kenyan small business owners (fast-food joints, poultry farms, cyber-shops etc.); half received a GPT-4-powered business adviser via WhatsApp, the control group received an online business-training guide — [Berkeley Haas](https://newsroom.haas.berkeley.edu/research/gen-ai-experiment-shows-mixed-results-in-helping-small-businesses-grow/); [HBS Working Knowledge](https://www.library.hbs.edu/working-knowledge/what-happens-when-business-owners-turn-to-chatbots-for-advice)
- Key findings: average effect on revenue/profit not statistically distinguishable from zero; treatment effect for baseline high performers was about 0.20–0.27 SD greater than for low performers; press summaries report roughly +15% for high performers and about −8% for low performers — [Columbia Business School](https://business.columbia.edu/insights/ai-widening-gap-between-high-and-low-performers); [Berkeley Haas](https://newsroom.haas.berkeley.edu/research/gen-ai-experiment-shows-mixed-results-in-helping-small-businesses-grow/)
- Mechanism: the divergence did not come from differences in the questions asked or the advice given, but from which advice entrepreneurs chose to implement; struggling owners asked for help on harder, more open-ended problems — [Columbia Business School](https://business.columbia.edu/insights/ai-widening-gap-between-high-and-low-performers); [MIT Sloan Management Review](https://www.mitsloanme.com/?p=12858)
- Plain-language summary: Asked whether a cheap AI "business mentor" helps real micro-businesses make more money. Gave 640 Kenyan owners a GPT-4 WhatsApp adviser for six months and compared sales and profits with a control group. On average nothing changed, but already-strong owners gained ~15% while weaker owners lost ~8%, because weaker owners acted on riskier advice for harder problems. For a future beverage-shop founder the lesson is that AI advice is only as good as the owner's judgement about which suggestions to implement.
- Classroom hook: the "AI widens the gap" result is counter-intuitive and the setting (WhatsApp chatbot for food stalls) is concrete and relatable. Difficulty: medium (intro is plain English; the econometrics can be skipped). Citation count: not visible in retrieved sources.

#### Paper 2 — Hartmann, Exner & Domdey — "The power of generative marketing: Can generative AI create superhuman visual marketing content?"
- Journal: International Journal of Research in Marketing, vol. 42(1), pp. 13–31, 2025; DOI 10.1016/j.ijresmar.2024.09.002 (RePEc record); ScienceDirect PII S0167811624000843 — [IDEAS/RePEc](https://ideas.repec.org/a/eee/ijrema/v42y2025i1p13-31.html); [TUM news](https://www.msl.mgt.tum.de/en/dm/news/article/genai-labs-ijrm-publication-ranking-as-most-downloaded-paper)
- Open access: publisher version is subscription-only per RePEc; a free earlier version exists as SSRN working paper DOI 10.2139/ssrn.4597899 (TUM mediaTUM record) — [mediaTUM](https://mediatum.ub.tum.de/1734298); German-language summary PDF — [121watt](https://121watt.de/wp-content/uploads/2024/12/Zusammenfassung_-Studie-„The-Power-of-Generative-Marketing.pdf)
- Method: seven text-to-image models (DALL-E 3, Midjourney v6, Firefly 2, Imagen 2, Imagine, Stable Diffusion XL Turbo, Realistic Vision) generated 10,320 synthetic marketing images from 2,400 human-made inputs; three studies: perception ratings, comparison with freelancers executing briefs, and A/B click-through tests in two Meta campaigns with 173,022 impressions and 907 clicks — [TUM story](https://www.mgt.tum.de/our-stories-with-impact/detail/the-power-of-generative-ai-in-visual-marketing)
- Findings: AI imagery surpassed human-made visuals in quality, realism and advertising effectiveness across >250,000 human ratings and 173,000 real impressions, with cost efficiencies and higher click-through rates; the authors released the GenImageNet dataset — [TUM story](https://www.mgt.tum.de/our-stories-with-impact/detail/the-power-of-generative-ai-in-visual-marketing)
- Recognition: IJRM's most downloaded article over a 90-day window (TUM report) — [TUM news](https://www.msl.mgt.tum.de/en/dm/news/article/genai-labs-ijrm-publication-ranking-as-most-downloaded-paper)
- Plain-language summary: Tests whether free/cheap image generators can replace stock photos and freelance designers for ads. Thousands of AI pictures were rated against real marketing images and run as live Facebook/Instagram ads. AI images were rated higher and got more clicks at far lower cost. For a small shop this is direct evidence that a founder can produce competitive social-media visuals in-house.
- Classroom hook: show real AI vs human ad images and ask the class to guess which performed better. Difficulty: medium. Citation count: not visible.

#### Paper 3 — Reisenbichler, Reutterer, Schweidel & Dan — "Frontiers: Supporting Content Marketing with Natural Language Generation"
- Journal: Marketing Science, vol. 41(3), pp. 441–452, 2022; DOI 10.1287/mksc.2022.1354 — [Modul University record](https://research.modul.ac.at/en/publications/frontiers-supporting-content-marketing-with-natural-language-gene/); [IDEAS/RePEc](https://ideas.repec.org/a/inm/ormksc/v41y2022i3p441-452.html)
- Open access: an earlier MSI working-paper version (Feb 2021) is freely described on MSI — [MSI working paper](https://www.msi.org/working-papers/supporting-content-marketing-with-natural-language-generation-2/); publisher OA status not confirmed in search results.
- Method and findings: a semi-automated "content writing machine" takes a target keyword and generates human-like SEO landing-page text; field experiments in two industries showed machine-written content outranked text by human writers, including SEO experts, in search results, and substantially reduced content production cost — [Modul University record](https://research.modul.ac.at/en/publications/frontiers-supporting-content-marketing-with-natural-language-gene/)
- Follow-up by same team: "Applying Large Language Models for Sponsored Search Advertising", Marketing Science 45(1), 123–141 (2025) — [WU Vienna research page](https://www.wu.ac.at/en/mca/research)
- Plain-language summary: Before ChatGPT, the authors built a GPT-based writer for website text and tested it live against professional copywriters. The machine's pages ranked higher on Google and cost much less to produce. For a shop owner who is also a programmer, it shows that a lightweight generate-then-rank pipeline can beat hiring an SEO agency.
- Classroom hook: "a robot beat the SEO experts at their own game" in a top marketing journal, pre-ChatGPT. Difficulty: medium-hard (technical NLG section; intro readable). Citation count: not visible.

#### Paper 4 — Shepherd & Majchrzak — "Machines augmenting entrepreneurs: Opportunities (and threats) at the Nexus of artificial intelligence and entrepreneurship"
- Journal: Journal of Business Venturing, vol. 37(4), article 106227, July 2022 — [Hanken research portal](https://harisportal.hanken.fi/en/publications/machines-augmenting-entrepreneurs-opportunities-and-threats-at-th/)
- DOI: 10.1016/j.jbusvent.2022.106227 — DOI not seen verbatim – verify before printing. Inference: neighbouring JBV article numbers 106204 (2022) and 106270 both carry the "j.jbusvent.2022" prefix, so 106227 should as well — [IDEAS JBV listing](https://ideas.repec.org/s/eee/jbvent2.html)
- Citations: Scopus counts shown on the Hanken portal pages ranged from 135 to 207 depending on capture date — [Hanken portal](https://harisportal.hanken.fi/en/publications/machines-augmenting-entrepreneurs-opportunities-and-threats-at-th/)
- Open access: not confirmed; no free PDF located.
- Content: argues AI plus entrepreneurship is a "super tool"; entrepreneurs can use AI to augment decisions and actions when pursuing opportunities; discusses the entrepreneur's tasks in developing and managing AI, dark-side risks, and a future research agenda — [Hanken portal](https://harisportal.hanken.fi/en/publications/machines-augmenting-entrepreneurs-opportunities-and-threats-at-th/)
- Plain-language summary: A conceptual paper by two leading entrepreneurship scholars asking how AI changes the way founders spot and pursue opportunities. No data; it builds a framework of where AI helps (information processing, decision support) and where it threatens (bias, over-reliance). Useful to a founder as a map of the decisions AI should and should not be trusted with.
- Classroom hook: most-cited theory piece in the field; good for a "big picture" opening. Difficulty: medium (long, conceptual).

#### Paper 5 — Chalmers, MacKenzie & Carter — "Artificial Intelligence and Entrepreneurship: Implications for Venture Creation in the Fourth Industrial Revolution"
- Journal: Entrepreneurship Theory and Practice, vol. 45(5), pp. 1028–1053, 2021 (online 1 July 2020); DOI 10.1177/1042258720934581 — [Strathclyde repository](https://strathprints.strath.ac.uk/90176); [IDEAS/RePEc](https://ideas.repec.org/a/sae/entthe/v45y2021i5p1028-1053.html)
- Open access: accepted-manuscript PDF free from Strathprints — [Strathprints PDF](https://strathprints.strath.ac.uk/90176/7/Chalmers-et-al-ETP-2020-Artificial-intelligence-and-entrepreneurship-implications-for-venture-creation.pdf)
- Content: examines how AI may change new-venture processes, practices and outcomes; how AI could augment or replace tasks in idea production, selling and scaling; proposes a research agenda on negative effects, particularly for small traditional firms at risk of being disintermediated — [Strathclyde repository](https://strathprints.strath.ac.uk/90176)
- Plain-language summary: Asks what happens to the classic startup journey (idea, sell, scale) when AI takes over parts of each step. It is a conceptual essay, warning that small traditional firms could be bypassed by AI-enabled platforms. For a founder it frames which parts of a new venture AI can realistically do.
- Classroom hook: the "small traditional firms at risk" warning is a provocative counterpoint to the hype. Difficulty: medium. Citation count: not visible.

- Journal: International Journal of Entrepreneurial Behavior & Research, vol. 29(4), pp. 816–837; DOI 10.1108/IJEBR-05-2021-0426 (year listed as 2022 by the university repository and 2023 by some citing works) — [Emerald PDF link](https://emerald.com/insight/content/doi/10.1108/IJEBR-05-2021-0426/full/pdf); [Tor Vergata repository](https://art.torvergata.it/handle/2108/306343)

#### Paper 7 — Upadhyay, Upadhyay & Dwivedi — "Theorizing artificial intelligence acceptance and digital entrepreneurship model"
- Journal: International Journal of Entrepreneurial Behavior & Research, vol. 28(5), pp. 1138–1166; article published 20 Sept 2021, issue 8 July 2022; DOI 10.1108/IJEBR-01-2021-0052 — [Emerald article page](https://www.emerald.com/ijebr/article/28/5/1138/125490/Theorizing-artificial-intelligence-acceptance-and); [Swansea Cronfa record](https://cronfa.swansea.ac.uk/Record/cronfa56711)
- Open access: accepted manuscript free under CC BY-NC 4.0 at Swansea Cronfa (3,900+ downloads shown) — [Cronfa PDF](https://cronfa.swan.ac.uk/Record/cronfa56711/Download/56711__19718__3547d55cd0f94890a7e3a87b88f8f988.pdf)
- Method/findings: UTAUT-style "AIADE" model tested on 476 entrepreneur responses (India); performance expectancy, openness, social influence, hedonic motivation and generativity raise intention to accept AI; affordance works only indirectly via attitude; inconvenience lowers and uncertainty raises intention; effort expectancy not significant — [Cronfa record](https://cronfa.swansea.ac.uk/Record/cronfa56711)
- Plain-language summary: A survey of 476 entrepreneurs asking what makes them willing to adopt AI. Expected usefulness, peer influence and enjoyment matter; perceived effort does not. For a founder: your peers' usage and visible payoff drive adoption more than ease of use.
- Classroom hook: textbook UTAUT applied to entrepreneurs, with a surprising "uncertainty increases adoption" result. Difficulty: medium (SEM statistics). Citation count: not visible.

#### Paper 9 — Chummee — "A Structural Model of Factors Influencing AI Adaptation and SME Performance in Bangkok and Nonthaburi, Thailand"
- Journal: Asia Social Issues (Walailak University, TCI-indexed), vol. 19(5), 2026; DOI 10.48048/asi.2026.290851; received 12 Dec 2025, accepted 2 Mar 2026, published 7 Apr 2026 — [article page](https://so06.tci-thaijo.org/index.php/asi/article/view/290851); [PDF](https://so06.tci-thaijo.org/index.php/asi/article/download/290851/194002)
- Open access: yes (free PDF on ThaiJO).
- Method/findings: extended TOE framework; simple random sample of 360 SME owners/managers; CFA and SEM; Technology β=0.36, Organization β=0.42, Environment β=0.29 on AI adaptation; AI adaptation → SME performance β=0.67 and mediates the TOE–performance link; performance measured as productivity, cost efficiency, quality, market expansion, profitability, sustainability — [article page](https://so06.tci-thaijo.org/index.php/asi/article/view/290851)
- Plain-language summary: The only Thailand-specific peer-reviewed SME/AI study found. 360 Bangkok-area SME owners were surveyed; organisational readiness mattered most, and firms that adapted AI reported much better performance. Directly relevant for a Thai founder, though it is a self-reported cross-sectional survey in a regional journal.
- Classroom hook: local data, local setting; classmates will recognise the context. Difficulty: easy. Citation count: none yet (published April 2026).

### Inferences
- The only paper combining a top-tier journal, a true field experiment, free full text and a small-business setting is Otis et al.; it should be the default recommendation for the class report.
- Fully OA papers in strong journals are rare in this niche; the TOE/UTAUT survey papers (Badghish & Soomro; Chummee; Upadhyay et al.) are the easiest to obtain legally for printing.
- Citation counts could not be retrieved for most papers; by venue prestige and age, Shepherd & Majchrzak (2022), Chalmers et al. (2021), Giuggioli & Pellegrini (2022/23) and Kshetri et al. ## Key Question 2: Which papers have surprising or actionable findings?

### Takeaway
Four results are genuinely surprising: AI advice helped strong Kenyan entrepreneurs (+15%) but hurt weak ones (−8%); AI-generated ad images beat human stock photos on real Meta click-through; machine-written SEO pages outranked human SEO experts; and people prefer AI-written ad copy when blind but reward human authorship when told.

### Cited Findings
- AI business adviser raised high performers' outcomes ~15% and lowered low performers' ~8%, with a 0.20–0.27 SD gap, driven by which advice owners implemented — [Columbia Business School](https://business.columbia.edu/insights/ai-widening-gap-between-high-and-low-performers); [Berkeley Haas](https://newsroom.haas.berkeley.edu/research/gen-ai-experiment-shows-mixed-results-in-helping-small-businesses-grow/)
- AI-generated marketing images surpassed human-made visuals in quality, realism and ad effectiveness across >250,000 ratings and 173,000 live impressions, with higher CTR and lower cost — [TUM](https://www.mgt.tum.de/our-stories-with-impact/detail/the-power-of-generative-ai-in-visual-marketing)
- A related Hartmann study found AI-image display ads outperform human-image ads on CTR only if consumers cannot infer that AI generated the images — [TUM](https://www.mgt.tum.de/our-stories-with-impact/detail/the-power-of-generative-ai-in-visual-marketing)
- NLG-written SEO content outperformed human writers including SEO experts in search rankings in two industries — [Modul University](https://research.modul.ac.at/en/publications/frontiers-supporting-content-marketing-with-natural-language-gene/)
- Blind evaluators preferred ChatGPT-4 ad copy; disclosure of human authorship raised ratings ("human favoritism") while disclosure of AI did not lower them — [MIT Sloan](https://mitsloan.mit.edu/ideas-made-to-matter/study-gauges-how-people-perceive-ai-created-content)
- Among entrepreneurs, perceived effort expectancy did not affect AI acceptance, while uncertainty actually increased acceptance intention — [Cronfa](https://cronfa.swansea.ac.uk/Record/cronfa56711)
- Thai SMEs: organisational factors (β=0.42) outweighed technology (β=0.36) and environment (β=0.29) in driving AI adaptation; AI adaptation → performance β=0.67 — [Asia Social Issues](https://so06.tci-thaijo.org/index.php/asi/article/view/290851)
- Malaysia: among 226 SMEs, compatibility (β=0.353) and relative advantage (β=0.280) drove adoption of AI marketing tools, explaining only 20.8% of variance — [JTMT](https://jtmt.utem.edu.my/jtmt/article/view/6142)
- Thai SMEs (Chachoengsao, 2025): main barriers were lack of knowledge/skills (73.6%), budget constraints (68.4%) and data-security concerns (61.2%) — [TCI journal record](https://so18.tci-thaijo.org/index.php/CIM_Journal2/article/view/1256)
- Copilot release cut software startups' time-to-first-funding by ~20%, strongest for founders with more technical/managerial experience — [MPI](https://ip.mpg.de/en/publications/details/generative-ai-and-firm-level-productivity-evidence-from-startup-funding-dynamics.html)
- GenAI's effect on VC-funding likelihood is stronger for founders with technical background; effect on launches is larger for founders without managerial experience — [arXiv 2511.06545](https://arxiv.org/html/2511.06545v1)

### Inferences
- For a programmer-founder, the Copilot and founder-expertise working papers suggest technical skill is a complement to GenAI (bigger funding gains), while the Kenya RCT warns that judgement about which AI advice to act on is the binding constraint.
- The "disclosure" results (Hartmann; Zhang & Gosline) imply a small shop can use AI visuals and copy effectively but should think about whether to label them.

### Gaps
- No peer-reviewed field experiment on AI marketing assistants for Indian or Chinese SMEs was found; the closest are the Kenya RCT and the (unpublished) cross-border e-commerce platform experiment.
- No study measuring actual sales lift from GenAI social-media content for micro-retailers or local service businesses was found (Gastmann & Bastos and García Rellán et al. assess output quality, not sales).

## Key Question 3: Which have readable, self-contained introductions suitable for printing as a class report?

### Takeaway
Easiest-to-print introductions: Otis et al. (plain-English motivation about whether AI helps small firms), Giuggioli & Pellegrini (review), Badghish & Soomro, Chummee, Gastmann & Bastos and García Rellán et al.; hardest: Reisenbichler et al. ### Cited Findings

- Kshetri et al. state the gap plainly: scholars "have not paid attention to the transformative impacts GAI has on marketing activities" — [Cronfa](https://cronfa.swansea.ac.uk/Record/cronfa64660/Details)

## Key Question 4: Studies set in Thailand or Southeast Asia, or involving cafés/food retail

### Takeaway
Thailand has one directly relevant 2026 peer-reviewed SME/AI TOE study (Chummee, Asia Social Issues) plus several smaller TCI-journal and thesis studies; Southeast Asia adds Malaysian and Indonesian MSME AI-marketing adoption surveys in regional journals; the only café/food-retail marketing paper is a Spanish OA case study building a ChatGPT Instagram campaign for a food-service SME, while the Kenya RCT sample included fast-food joints.

### Cited Findings
- Thailand (peer-reviewed, OA): Chummee (2026), Asia Social Issues 19(5), DOI 10.48048/asi.2026.290851, 360 SME owners, Bangkok/Nonthaburi, extended TOE — [article](https://so06.tci-thaijo.org/index.php/asi/article/view/290851)

### Inferences
- For a Thai beverage-shop founder, pairing the Kenya RCT (rigorous, global) with Chummee (local, OA) would give a presentation both credibility and local resonance.

### Gaps
- No Vietnamese or Singaporean SME GenAI-marketing journal article surfaced.
- No peer-reviewed study on AI marketing specifically for cafés, coffee shops or beverage retailers was found in any country; the Redmarka food-service case is the nearest match.
