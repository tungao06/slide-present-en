## Key Question 1 — Which 2021–2026 papers study AI/robots/chatbots in cafés, coffee shops, beverage retail or QSRs, and are freely accessible?

### Takeaway
The richest, most café-specific literature is a Korean cluster on **robot barista coffee shops** (IJHM, IJCHM, JHTT, Service Business, Sustainability), plus restaurant service-robot studies in Thailand, Taiwan and China (IJCHM, JHMM). About half of the shortlist has a free full text (gold OA at MDPI/SEPS/IHR or green OA author manuscripts at UK/Finnish repositories).

### Cited Findings — THE SHORTLIST (12 primary candidates, ordered roughly by fit for a future beverage-shop owner)

**1. Human vs robot baristas during the COVID-19 pandemic: effects of masks and vaccines on perceived safety and visit intention**
- Authors: Miju Choi (Leeds Beckett), Youngjoon Choi (Ewha), Seongseop (Sam) Kim (HK PolyU), Frank Badu-Baiden (HK Metropolitan U). Journal: *International Journal of Contemporary Hospitality Management*, 2023, Vol. 35 No. 2, pp. 469–491 (online 1 Sep 2022). DOI: 10.1108/IJCHM-02-2022-0157 — [Emerald page](https://emerald.com/insight/content/doi/10.1108/IJCHM-02-2022-0157/full/html); [PolyU record](https://research.polyu.edu.hk/en/publications/human-vs-robot-baristas-during-the-covid-19-pandemic-effects-of-m/)
- **OA: YES (green) — accepted manuscript PDF free at Leeds Beckett:** [eprints.leedsbeckett.ac.uk PDF](https://eprints.leedsbeckett.ac.uk/id/eprint/9947/7/HumanVsRobotBaristasDuringTheCovid19PandemicEffectsOfMasksAndVaccinesOnPerceivedSafetyAndVisitIntentionAM-CHOI.pdf)
- Summary: Three scenario experiments compare how customers react to a human vs a robot barista. Perceived safety mediates the effect of barista type on visit intention; mask-wearing changes perceived safety differently for human vs robot baristas, and customers' vaccination status moderates these effects, especially where robots are used. Authors caution generalisability because COVID rules differed by country. [Search summary of abstract](https://eprints.leedsbeckett.ac.uk/id/eprint/9947/7/HumanVsRobotBaristasDuringTheCovid19PandemicEffectsOfMasksAndVaccinesOnPerceivedSafetyAndVisitIntentionAM-CHOI.pdf)
- Managerial implication for a small café: a robot barista is a "safety/hygiene" signal that can lift visit intention in health-anxious periods, but the benefit depends on context (e.g., staff hygiene cues like masks); robots are a positioning choice, not just a labour substitute.
- Classroom appeal: directly answers "robot or human barista?", experimental design is easy to explain with a 2×2 picture; COVID framing is familiar to everyone.
- Difficulty: **Easy–Medium**. Citations: not retrievable (gap).

**2. Barista robots with human appeal: unraveling the impact of anthropomorphism, human presence, and perceived financial constraints on consumer behavior**
- Authors: Heewon Kim, Soona Park, Jaehee Gim, Sung In Kim. Journal: *International Journal of Hospitality Management*, 2024, Vol. 122, Article 103849, pp. 1–11. DOI: 10.1016/j.ijhm.2024.103849 — [HCU Hamburg catalogue record](https://katalog.hcu-hamburg.de/vufind/Search2Record/1905317913)
- OA: **Not confirmed** (Elsevier; no repository copy found). 
- Summary: Studies how a barista robot's human-likeness (anthropomorphism), the presence of a human employee alongside the robot, and customers' perceived financial constraints shape consumer responses; keywords point to mechanisms of "entitativity" and "perceived effort" (the robot being seen to work hard for the customer). Full findings were not retrievable in this environment — only title/keywords/abstract opening confirmed. [Record](https://katalog.hcu-hamburg.de/vufind/Search2Record/1905317913)
- Managerial implication: suggests design decisions (how human-like the robot looks; whether a human barista stays visible) interact with price-sensitivity of the customer base — relevant to whether a budget café vs premium café should "humanise" its robot.
- Classroom appeal: very recent, top hospitality journal, provocative question (should a robot look human?).
- Difficulty: **Medium** (experimental/psychological constructs). Citations: gap. **Verify findings before choosing.**

**3. Consequences of brand modernity in the coffee industry: the moderating role of human barista and robot barista**
- Authors: Jinkyung Jenny Kim, Heather Markham Kim, Jinsoo Hwang. Journal: *Journal of Hospitality and Tourism Technology*, 2022, Vol. 13 No. 5, pp. 801–815 (published 15/30 Nov 2022). DOI: 10.1108/JHTT-08-2021-0239 — [Emerald page](https://www.emerald.com/insight/content/doi/10.1108/JHTT-08-2021-0239/full/html)
- OA: **No** (paywalled; no repository copy found).
- Summary: Field survey in two real Korean coffee shops — 333 customers at a robot-barista shop and 354 at a human-barista shop. Brand modernity (being seen as modern/innovative) raises brand attitude, which raises brand attachment and brand loyalty; the barista type (robot vs human) moderates these links. [Emerald abstract via search](https://www.emerald.com/insight/content/doi/10.1108/JHTT-08-2021-0239/full/html)
- Managerial implication: for a new café, a robot barista is primarily a *brand-modernity* device — it can build attitude/loyalty if the brand story is "modern", but the effect differs from a human-staffed shop.
- Classroom appeal: real-shop comparison (not hypothetical), simple path model, coffee-specific.
- Difficulty: **Easy–Medium**. Citations: gap.

**4. The role of experiential value, novelty, and satisfaction in robot barista coffee shop in South Korea: COVID-19 crisis and beyond**
- Authors: Seon Hee Kim, Se Ran Yoo, Hyeon Mo Jeon. Journal: *Service Business* (Springer), 2022, Vol. 16 No. 3, pp. 771–790. DOI: 10.1007/s11628-021-00467-4 — [RePEc/IDEAS record](https://ideas.repec.org/a/spr/svcbiz/v16y2022i3d10.1007_s11628-021-00467-4.html)
- OA: **No** (RePEc notes restricted access).
- Summary: Survey of 300 customers of a robot-barista coffee shop (RBCS) in Korea. Atmosphere, novelty and "consumer return on investment" (value for money/time) drive satisfaction and behavioural intention. [IDEAS record](https://ideas.repec.org/a/spr/svcbiz/v16y2022i3d10.1007_s11628-021-00467-4.html)
- Managerial implication: the robot's novelty wears off; atmosphere and perceived value-for-money matter most for repeat visits — so a robot café still needs interior design and fair pricing.
- Classroom appeal: directly about a robot coffee shop; easy constructs (atmosphere, novelty, satisfaction).
- Difficulty: **Easy**. Citations: gap.

**5. Examining Image Congruence and Its Consequences in the Context of Robotic Coffee Shops**
- Authors: Heather Markham Kim, Kisang Ryu (Sejong University). Journal: *Sustainability* (MDPI), 2021, Vol. 13 No. 20, Article 11413 (published 15 Oct 2021). DOI: 10.3390/su132011413 — [MDPI PDF](https://mdpi-res.com/d_attachment/sustainability/sustainability-13-11413/article_deploy/sustainability-13-11413-v2.pdf)
- **OA: YES (gold, CC BY).** Free PDF at the MDPI link above.
- Summary: 325 customers of robotic coffee shops. Actual-, ideal- and social-self-image congruence with the robotic café each raise brand attitude; brand attitude raises brand attachment and loyalty. [MDPI PDF](https://mdpi-res.com/d_attachment/sustainability/sustainability-13-11413/article_deploy/sustainability-13-11413-v2.pdf)
- Managerial implication: a robot café attracts customers whose self-image is "tech-savvy/modern"; target segments and brand imagery accordingly.
- Classroom appeal: self-image congruence is intuitive and discussable ("is a robot café 'me'?"); fully free to print.
- Difficulty: **Easy**. Citations: gap. (MDPI venue — include only with caveat; it is among the earliest robot-café studies.)

**6. The Emergence of Service Robots at Restaurants: Integrating Trust, Perceived Risk, and Satisfaction**
- Authors: Kyung Hwa Seo, Jee Hye Lee. Journal: *Sustainability* (MDPI), 2021, Vol. 13 No. 8, Article 4431 (April 2021). DOI: 10.3390/su13084431 — [MDPI record](https://www.mdpi.com/2071-1050/13/8/4431/xml); [RePEc](https://ideas.repec.org/a/gam/jsusta/v13y2021i8p4431-d536991.html)
- **OA: YES (gold).**
- Summary: Scenario-based online survey (n = 338) extending TAM with trust, perceived risk and satisfaction. Trust raises perceived usefulness/ease of use, reduces perceived risk and raises satisfaction; risk lowers satisfaction and revisit intention; usefulness directly drives revisit intention while ease of use only works indirectly. [Ulsan repository](https://oak.ulsan.ac.kr/handle/2021.oak/9590)
- Managerial implication: build trust (reliability, visible accuracy) before worrying about how "easy" the robot is; address risk perceptions (hygiene, errors) explicitly.
- Classroom appeal: TAM is MBA-familiar; clear boxes-and-arrows model.
- Difficulty: **Easy**. Citations: described in search results as frequently cited in later reviews (exact count: gap).

**7. You don't have to tip the human waiters anymore, but … Unveiling factors that influence consumers' willingness to pay a price premium for robotic restaurants** — **THAILAND**
- Authors: Stephanie Hui-Wen Chuah, Siriprapha Jitanugoon, Pittinun Puntha, Eugene Cheng-Xi Aw. Journal: *International Journal of Contemporary Hospitality Management*, 2022, Vol. 34 No. 10, pp. 3553–3587 (online 17 May 2022). DOI: 10.1108/IJCHM-08-2021-1023 — [Emerald page](https://emerald.com/insight/content/doi/10.1108/IJCHM-08-2021-1023/full/html)
- OA: **No** (no free copy found).
- Summary: Survey of **897 Thai consumers** who had eaten at a robotic restaurant in the previous 12 months (SEM, chi-square, ANOVA). Demographics (gender, age, income, marital status), psychographics (perceived pros/cons, innovativeness, personality) and situational factors (perceived health risk, self-protective behaviour) all significantly affect willingness to pay a price premium; those willing to pay more have distinct profiles from neutral/unwilling consumers. [Emerald abstract](https://emerald.com/insight/content/doi/10.1108/IJCHM-08-2021-1023/full/html)
- Managerial implication: in Thailand there *is* a segment willing to pay extra for robot service — a small operator can identify it by age/income/innovativeness and price accordingly.
- Classroom appeal: Thai data, large sample, pricing angle (can a robot justify a higher price?).
- Difficulty: **Medium** (long paper, many variables). Citations: gap.

**9. Robotic service quality, authenticity, and revisit intention to restaurants in China: extending cognitive appraisal theory** — **CHINA**
- Authors: Hanqun Song, Huijun Yang, Erose Sthapit. Journal: *International Journal of Contemporary Hospitality Management*, Vol. 37 No. 5, pp. 1497–1515 (online 2023; issue 2025). DOI: 10.1108/IJCHM-11-2022-1396 — [MMU e-space record](https://e-space.mmu.ac.uk/632780); [Essex repository](https://repository.essex.ac.uk/36444/)
- **OA: YES (green, CC BY-NC accepted manuscript):** [MMU PDF](https://e-space.mmu.ac.uk/632780/3/632780_Manuscript%20revised.pdf); [Essex PDF](https://repository.essex.ac.uk/36444/1/Robotic%20service%20quality%20-%20Manuscript.pdf)
- Summary: Web survey of 428 Chinese diners who had used robot service in restaurants (SEM + multigroup). Functional service quality raises robotic-service authenticity and existential authenticity; technical quality affects only existential authenticity, which drives revisit intention; robot appearance moderates the quality→authenticity link. Authors advise restaurateurs to improve robots' technical service with manufacturers/operators. [Essex record](https://repository.essex.ac.uk/36444/)
- Managerial implication: reliability/technical performance of the robot (not gimmickry) is what makes customers feel the experience is "real" and come back.
- Classroom appeal: China's robot-restaurant boom; "authenticity of a robot" is a fun paradox to debate.
- Difficulty: **Medium–Hard** (cognitive appraisal theory). Citations: gap.

**10. Friendly or competent? The effects of perception of robot appearance and service context on usage intention**
- Authors: Xing (Stella) Liu, Xiao (Shannon) Yi, Lisa C. Wan. Journal: *Annals of Tourism Research*, 2022, Vol. 92, Article 103324. DOI: 10.1016/j.annals.2021.103324 — [RePEc record](https://ideas.repec.org/a/eee/anture/v92y2022ics0160738321002024.html); [OUCI record](https://ouci.dntb.gov.ua/works/408Lpj54)
- OA: **No** (paywalled; no repository copy found). Won 3rd prize, 2022 ATR video-abstract competition (a video abstract exists).
- Summary: Four studies show a congruity effect: customers prefer a *warm/friendly-looking* robot in hedonic contexts (e.g., leisure dining, cafés) and a *competent-looking* robot in utilitarian contexts (e.g., quick functional service); trust is the mechanism. [RePEc abstract](https://ideas.repec.org/a/eee/anture/v92y2022ics0160738321002024.html)
- Managerial implication: a cosy café should pick a cute/warm robot; a grab-and-go kiosk should pick an efficient-looking one.
- Classroom appeal: visual (show two robot pictures and let classmates vote), top-tier journal, memorable 2×2 result.
- Difficulty: **Medium–Hard** (multi-study experiments). Citations: gap.

**11. Spicing up hospitality service encounters: the case of Pepper™** — **OB / EMPLOYEE angle, restaurant/food-service setting**
- Authors: Aarni Tuomi, Iis P. Tussyadiah, Paul Hanna. Journal: *International Journal of Contemporary Hospitality Management*, 2021, Vol. 33 No. 11 (pages not confirmed). DOI: 10.1108/IJCHM-07-2020-0739 — [Emerald page](https://emerald.com/insight/content/doi/10.1108/IJCHM-07-2020-0739); [Surrey repository](https://openresearch.surrey.ac.uk/esploro/outputs/99542622902346)
- **OA: YES (green):** [Theseus (Haaga-Helia) PDF](https://theseus.fi/bitstream/handle/10024/495869/TuomiAIisPTussyadiahPSpicinguphospitality.pdf?sequence=1)
- Summary: Qualitative multi-method study prototyping SoftBank's Pepper as (a) information provider and (b) order-taker in food service; evaluated with 30 hospitality experts and 18 frontline employees via observation, interviews and photo-elicitation. Adoption is shaped by contextual, social, interactional and psychological determinants; robots shift the encounter from technical to social/psychological concerns (self-esteem, social judgment); routine unpleasant tasks (repeat orders, complaints) expected to go to robots or human–robot teams. [Theseus PDF](https://theseus.fi/bitstream/handle/10024/495869/TuomiAIisPTussyadiahPSpicinguphospitality.pdf?sequence=1)
- Managerial implication: use a robot to *support* staff on repetitive tasks (greeting, order-taking) rather than replace them; prepare staff for the social dynamics.
- Classroom appeal: photos of Pepper taking orders; qualitative quotes from staff; readable introduction.
- Difficulty: **Medium** (qualitative, conceptual). Citations: gap.

**12. Are robots stealing our jobs? Examining robot-phobia as a job stressor in the hospitality workplace** — **OB / EMPLOYEE angle, incl. food service**
- Authors: Chun-Chu (Bamboo) Chen, Ruiying/Ruying Cai (spelling varies across sources). Journal: *International Journal of Contemporary Hospitality Management*, 2024 (ahead-of-print 30 Apr 2024; volume/issue **not confirmed**). DOI: 10.1108/IJCHM-09-2023-1454 — [Emerald page](https://Www.Emeraldinsight.com/insight/content/doi/10.1108/IJCHM-09-2023-1454/full/html); [WSU press release](https://news.wsu.edu/press-release/2024/05/20/robot-phobia-could-exasperate-hotel-restaurant-labor-shortage/)
- OA: **No** (press coverage only).
- Summary: US survey of 321 lodging and 308 food-service employees (SEM, t-tests, multigroup). "Robot-phobia" (fear robots take jobs) raises job insecurity and stress, which raise turnover intention; effects are stronger for staff with real robot experience and apply to managers too. "Robots" defined broadly, incl. kiosks and tabletop devices. Authors recommend training on robots' pros/cons and a collaborative human–robot culture. [WSU release](https://news.wsu.edu/press-release/2024/05/20/robot-phobia-could-exasperate-hotel-restaurant-labor-shortage/)
- Managerial implication: even a self-order kiosk can trigger staff anxiety and quitting — communicate and involve staff when automating a small shop.
- Classroom appeal: counter-intuitive headline (automation worsens labour shortage), strong press coverage, OB fit.
- Difficulty: **Medium**. Citations: gap.

## Key Question 2 — Which papers have findings a future café owner could act on (robot vs human preference, AI recommendations lifting basket size, AI forecasting cutting waste)?

### Takeaway
Robot-vs-human preference is well covered (items 1, 3, 7, 8, 10, 11). **AI forecasting to cut waste** has one solid OA peer-reviewed paper (Malefors et al. 2022). **AI recommendation lifting basket size** has NO verified 2021+ peer-reviewed hospitality paper — only industry claims (McDonald's/Dynamic Yield, DoorDash) and one unverified lab study.

### Cited Findings
- **Forecasting/waste:** *Food waste reduction and economic savings in times of crisis: The potential of machine learning methods to plan guest attendance in Swedish public catering during the Covid-19 pandemic* — Christopher Malefors, Luca Secondi, Stefano Marchetti, Mattias Eriksson. *Socio-Economic Planning Sciences*, 2022, Vol. 82, Article 101041 (online 2 Mar 2021). DOI: 10.1016/j.seps.2021.101041. **OA: YES (CC BY)** — [PMC full text](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9675941); [SLU repository](https://pub.epsilon.slu.se/28757/). Random forest beat a simple ANN for predicting daily guest attendance (conditional MAPE < 0.15 on training data); forecasting yielded economic savings vs no-plan scenario and matters most when demand is volatile. Setting is school/preschool catering, not a café — but the method transfers to daily drink/pastry production planning. Difficulty: **Medium–Hard** (technical; fine for a programmer). [RePEc record](https://ideas.repec.org/a/eee/soceps/v82y2022ipas0038012121000331.html)
- Robot vs human preference: warm robot for hedonic contexts, competent robot for utilitarian contexts (Liu, Yi & Wan 2022) — [RePEc](https://ideas.repec.org/a/eee/anture/v92y2022ics0160738321002024.html)
- Robot as safety cue during health scares (Choi et al. 2023) — [Leeds Beckett PDF](https://eprints.leedsbeckett.ac.uk/id/eprint/9947/7/HumanVsRobotBaristasDuringTheCovid19PandemicEffectsOfMasksAndVaccinesOnPerceivedSafetyAndVisitIntentionAM-CHOI.pdf)
- Robot novelty fades; atmosphere and value-for-money drive satisfaction in robot cafés (Kim, Yoo & Jeon 2022) — [IDEAS](https://ideas.repec.org/a/spr/svcbiz/v16y2022i3d10.1007_s11628-021-00467-4.html)
- Thai consumers: identifiable segment willing to pay a premium for robot service (Chuah et al. 2022) — [Emerald](https://emerald.com/insight/content/doi/10.1108/IJCHM-08-2021-1023/full/html)
- Staff side: automation (even kiosks) raises job insecurity and turnover intention unless managed (Chen & Cai 2024) — [WSU](https://news.wsu.edu/press-release/2024/05/20/robot-phobia-could-exasperate-hotel-restaurant-labor-shortage/); employees report reduced physical strain but frustration with reliability (Mejia et al. 2024) — [UCF](https://stars.library.ucf.edu/rosen-research-review/vol6/iss1/11)
- **AI menu/recommendation (industry, not peer-reviewed):** McDonald's rolled out Dynamic Yield personalised recommendations to 12,000+ US drive-thrus, A/B-tested against alternative algorithms — [Mastercard](https://www.mastercard.com/us/en/news-and-trends/Insights/2023/mcdonalds.html); DoorDash reported customers shown personalised recommendations were >25% more likely to order (email campaigns) — [VentureBeat](https://venturebeat.com/ai/doordash-sees-25-lift-from-ai-recommendations). - **Voice AI ordering:** no peer-reviewed behavioural-intention study found; trade evidence is mixed — Burger King exec: "guests don't want to talk to a bot", Technomic: 23% find AI drive-thru appetising vs 46% a turn-off — [Entrepreneur](https://entrepreneur.com/buying-a-franchise/burger-king-is-rethinking-ai-drive-thrus-guests-dont-want-to-talk-to-a-bot)

### Inferences
