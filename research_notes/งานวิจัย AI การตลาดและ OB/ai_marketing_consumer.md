#### 1. Longoni & Cian (2022) — "Artificial Intelligence in Utilitarian vs. Hedonic Contexts: The 'Word-of-Machine' Effect"
- **Authors:** Chiara Longoni, Luca Cian. **Journal:** Journal of Marketing, 2022, vol. 86, issue 1, pp. 91–108 (online first 2020). — [Bocconi faculty page](https://faculty.unibocconi.eu/chiaralongoni); [SDA Bocconi](https://www.sdabocconi.it/en/faculty-research/faculty-knowledge/chiara-longoni/)
- **DOI:** 10.1177/0022242920957347 — *unverified in this session (from researcher knowledge; the search tool could not confirm the DOI string). Check at https://doi.org/10.1177/0022242920957347.*
- **Open access:** No free PDF confirmed. Longoni's Bocconi CV lists a "[paper]" link; no SSRN/ResearchGate copy was found. — [Bocconi CV](https://marketing.unibocconi.eu/sites/default/files/media/cv/Chiara%20Longoni_CV_13.pdf). Press summaries that explain the findings are free: [AMA press release](https://www.ama.org/2020/11/24/when-consumers-trust-ai-recommendations-or-resist-them/); [Darden Ideas to Action](https://ideas.darden.virginia.edu/word-of-machine-effect).
- **Summary:** Research question: when do consumers trust an AI recommender versus a human recommender? Method: a series of lab/online experiments plus a field-style study (recommendations for products such as food and consumer goods). Key finding: consumers prefer AI recommenders when they focus on a product's utilitarian/functional value and prefer human recommenders when they focus on hedonic/experiential value; the "word-of-machine" effect reverses when AI is framed as augmenting a human expert. — [Newswise/AMA summary](https://www.newswise.com/articles/when-consumers-trust-ai-recommendations-or-resist-them); [Darden](https://ideas.darden.virginia.edu/word-of-machine-marketing-recommendations). Managerial implication: match the recommender type (AI vs. human vs. AI-assisted human) to whether the pitch is about function or pleasure.
- **Recognition:** Won the Consumer Behavior SIG "Research in Practice Best Paper Award" (2022); the authors also won a 2026 AMA AI award. — [BU DBI](https://www.bu.edu/dbi/?p=1619); [Bocconi news](https://marketing.unibocconi.eu/news/chiara-longoni-and-co-authors-win-2026-american-marketing-association-artificial-intelligence)
- **Why engaging:** Very relatable 2x2 (function vs. pleasure; AI vs. human); food/gift examples; one simple bar chart tells the story. **Difficulty:** Easy–Medium. **Citations:** not retrievable this session (gap); award-winning and widely cited per AMA/Bocconi pages.
- **Food/café bonus:** Yes — the paper's experiments use food and hedonic consumer goods (e.g., chocolate/recipe-type recommendations) as stimuli (researcher knowledge; the specific stimuli were not confirmable from search snippets).

#### 2. Garvey, Kim & Duhachek (2023) — "Bad News? Send an AI. Good News? Send a Human"
- **Authors:** Aaron M. Garvey, TaeWoo Kim, Adam Duhachek. **Journal:** Journal of Marketing, 2023, vol. 87, issue 1, pp. 10–25 (online first 2021/2022). **DOI:** 10.1177/00222429211066972 — **verified** via [University of Kentucky Scholars](https://scholars.uky.edu/en/publications/bad-news-send-an-ai-good-news-send-a-human/) and [AMA press release](https://www.ama.org/2022/02/09/press-release-from-the-journal-of-marketing-bad-news-send-an-ai-good-news-send-a-human/).
- **Open access:** A repository record exists at UTS OPUS ([opus.cloud1.lib.uts.edu.au/handle/10453/175862](https://opus.cloud1.lib.uts.edu.au/handle/10453/175862)) which typically hosts the accepted manuscript — *whether the PDF is downloadable was not confirmable in this session.* Free plain-language summaries: [AMA](https://www.ama.org/2022/02/09/bad-news-send-an-ai-good-news-send-a-human/); [UKY Gatton](https://gatton.uky.edu/news/bad-news-send-ai-good-news-send-human).
- **Summary:** Research question: should a company let an AI agent or a human deliver a product offer? Method: multiple experiments. Key finding: when an offer is worse than expected, consumers react better (higher purchase likelihood and satisfaction) when it comes from an AI; when the offer is better than expected, they react better to a human. The mechanism is perceived intent: AI is seen as having weaker intentions, so it gets less blame for bad offers but also less credit for good ones; anthropomorphizing the AI strengthens perceived intent. — [AMA press release](https://www.ama.org/2022/02/09/press-release-from-the-journal-of-marketing-bad-news-send-an-ai-good-news-send-a-human/); [UKY Research](https://www.research.uky.edu/news/ai-bot-vs-live-agent-uk-researchers-examine-customer-responses). Managerial implication: route bad-news offers (price increases, out-of-stock substitutes) through bots and good news through people, but the authors flag ethical concerns about using AI to bypass consumer resistance.
- **Why engaging:** The title alone is a hook; counter-intuitive and immediately applicable to any customer-service team. **Difficulty:** Easy. **Citations:** not retrievable (gap).

#### 3. Crolic, Thomaz, Hadi & Stephen (2022) — "Blame the Bot: Anthropomorphism and Anger in Customer–Chatbot Interactions"
- **Authors:** Cammy Crolic, Felipe Thomaz, Rhonda Hadi, Andrew T. Stephen. **Journal:** Journal of Marketing, 2022, vol. 86, issue 1, pp. 132–148 (online Aug 2021). **DOI:** 10.1177/00222429211045687 — **verified** ([ablesci record](https://www.ablesci.com/assist/detail?id=weAV55); [ServSIG](https://www.servsig.org/wordpress/?p=11733)).
- **Open access:** **Free author manuscript in Oxford University Research Archive (ORA):** [ora.ox.ac.uk PDF](https://ora.ox.ac.uk/objects/uuid:73d46bba-35d1-465c-be00-aa6f4f4ccb84/files/r5x21tf917). Also listed at [Saïd Business School publications](https://www.sbs.ox.ac.uk/publications/4466).
- **Summary:** Research question: does making a customer-service chatbot more human-like always help? Method: one analysis of a large real-world dataset from a telecom company plus four experiments. Key finding: when customers enter the chat already angry, a human-like chatbot *lowers* satisfaction, firm evaluations and purchase intentions; no such effect for non-angry customers. Mechanism: expectancy violation — human-like cues inflate expectations the bot cannot meet. — [ORA abstract](https://ora.ox.ac.uk/objects/uuid:73d46bba-35d1-465c-be00-aa6f4f4ccb84/files/r5x21tf917); [napstic summary](https://search.napstic.cn/literature/periodical/0220220600639464). Managerial implication: de-humanize bots (or route to humans) in complaint/problem-resolution contexts.
- **Why engaging:** Everyone has yelled at a chatbot; mix of real company data and experiments; clear interaction chart (anger × anthropomorphism). **Difficulty:** Easy–Medium. **Citations:** not retrievable (gap).

#### 5. Zhang & Gosline (2023) — "Human Favoritism, Not AI Aversion: People's Perceptions (and Bias) Toward Generative AI, Human Experts, and Human–GAI Collaboration in Persuasive Content Generation"
- **Authors:** Yunhao Zhang, Renée Richardson Gosline (MIT Sloan). **Journal:** Judgment and Decision Making (Cambridge University Press), 2023, vol. 18, article e41. **DOI:** 10.1017/jdm.2023.37 — *venue/DOI from researcher knowledge; a Substack commentary attributes the paper to Cambridge University Press but the search tool could not confirm the journal record. Verify at https://doi.org/10.1017/jdm.2023.37.* — [MIT Sloan Ideas Made to Matter](https://mitsloan.mit.edu/ideas-made-to-matter/study-gauges-how-people-perceive-ai-created-content); [Christopher Penn newsletter](https://www.christopherspenn.com/2023/12/almost-timely-news-december-3-2023-ai-content-is-preferred-over-human-content/)
- **Open access:** Judgment and Decision Making is a fully open-access (CC BY) journal — *unverified in this session*; the MIT Sloan article is free.
- **Summary:** Research question: do people penalize marketing copy because it was made by generative AI? Method: experiments with Accenture testing four conditions (human only, AI then human-edited, human then AI-edited, pure AI) on two tasks — marketing copy for five retail products and persuasive messages for five campaigns (e.g., "eat less junk food"). - **Why engaging:** Directly tests the "made with AI" label question with surprising result; easy to replicate as a live class poll. **Difficulty:** Easy. **Citations:** not retrievable (gap).

#### 8. Matz, Teeny, Vaid, Peters, Harari & Cerf (2024) — "The potential of generative AI for personalized persuasion at scale"
- **Authors:** Sandra C. Matz, Jacob D. Teeny, Sumer S. Vaid, Heinrich Peters, Gabriella M. Harari, Moran Cerf. **Journal:** Scientific Reports, 2024, vol. 14, article 4692 (26 Feb 2024). **DOI:** 10.1038/s41598-024-53755-0 — **verified** ([Stanford MAP Lab](https://maplab.stanford.edu/publications/potential-generative-ai-personalized-persuasion-scale); [Kellogg](https://www.kellogg.northwestern.edu/academics-research/research/detail/2024/the-potential-of-generative-ai-for-personalized-persuasion-at/)).

#### 13. Baek, Kim & Kim (2024) — "Effect of disclosing AI-generated content on prosocial advertising evaluation"
- **Authors:** Tae Hyun Baek, Jungkeun Kim, Jeong Hyun Kim. **Journal:** International Journal of Advertising, published online 11 Sep 2024 (ahead-of-print at time of repository record; pp. 1–22). **DOI:** 10.1080/02650487.2024.2401319 — **verified** ([AUT Open Repository](https://openrepository.aut.ac.nz/items/43fb7863-ce26-4948-ac07-c44edfe2d9dc/full)).

Method: three experiments, grounded in the persuasion knowledge model and algorithm-aversion research. Managerial implication: if disclosing AI, protect credibility (e.g., explain why AI was used).
- **Why engaging:** Squarely the "AI label" question; easy stimuli to show. **Difficulty:** Easy. **Citations:** not retrievable (gap). Final volume/issue not yet confirmed (gap).

- **Kirk & Givi (2025)** — "The AI-authorship effect: Understanding authenticity, moral disgust, and consumer responses to AI-generated marketing communications," Journal of Business Research, vol. 186, article 114984, DOI 10.1016/j.jbusres.2024.114984 (**verified** via [RePEc](https://ideas.repec.org/a/eee/jbrese/v186y2025ics0148296324004880.html)). ## Key Question 2: Which papers have surprising or counter-intuitive findings suitable for a presentation?

### Takeaway
The most presentation-friendly "twists" are: bad news lands better from a bot (Garvey et al.), human-like chatbots backfire on angry customers (Crolic et al.), people cannot tell GPT-4 restaurant reviews from real ones and even trust the fakes more (Kovács), AI copy wins blind tests but loses once labeled (Zhang & Gosline; Brüns & Meißner), AI-generated SEO text beat human experts in the field (Reisenbichler et al.), and consumers dislike *good* news from an algorithm (Yalcin et al.).

### Cited Findings
- Worse-than-expected offers get higher purchase likelihood and satisfaction when delivered by AI; better-than-expected offers do better from a human. — [AMA/JM press release](https://www.ama.org/2022/02/09/press-release-from-the-journal-of-marketing-bad-news-send-an-ai-good-news-send-a-human/)
- Human-like chatbots lower satisfaction, firm evaluation and purchase intention specifically for customers who arrive angry (telecom field data + 4 experiments). — [ORA manuscript](https://ora.ox.ac.uk/objects/uuid:73d46bba-35d1-465c-be00-aa6f4f4ccb84/files/r5x21tf917)
- GPT-4-written restaurant reviews were judged human-written 64% of the time vs. 53% for actual human reviews; AI detectors also failed. — [Yale Insights](https://insights.som.yale.edu/insights/ai-can-write-more-believable-restaurant-review-than-human-can)
- Blind to source, people preferred AI-generated marketing copy; told the source, they favored humans but did not penalize AI ("human favoritism, not AI aversion"). — [MIT Sloan](https://mitsloan.mit.edu/ideas-made-to-matter/study-gauges-how-people-perceive-ai-created-content)
- Participants could not distinguish GenAI from human social-media content, yet disclosure of GenAI use reduced perceived brand authenticity and follower engagement. — [TUM](https://mgt.tum.de/our-stories-with-impact/detail/genai-diminishes-brand-authenticity)
- Machine-generated SEO content (with human editing) outperformed SEO experts' content in search rankings in two industries' field experiments. — [INFORMS abstract](https://pubsonline.informs.org/doi/fpi/10.1287/mksc.2022.1354)
- Consumers react less positively to a *favorable* decision made by an algorithm than by a human — the opposite of what managers predicted. — [Erasmus Pure record](https://pure.eur.nl/en/datasets/thumbs-up-or-down-consumer-reactions-to-decisions-by-algorithms-v/)
- Bot service is rated worse than identical human service because consumers assume cost-cutting; sharing the savings (discount) eliminates the penalty. — [U. Alberta](https://ualberta.ca/folio/2023/12/service-bots-turn-off-customers-even-when-they-work-as-well-as-humans)
- AI-generated images surpassed human-made marketing visuals on quality, realism and ad effectiveness across >250,000 ratings and ~173,000 real impressions. — [TUM](https://www.mgt.tum.de/our-stories-with-impact/stories/the-power-of-generative-ai-in-visual-marketing)
- ChatGPT-personalized messages were more persuasive than generic ones (N = 1,788) — but a later PNAS study found no gain from demographic personalization of political messages. — [PMC10897294](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10897294); contradicted in part by [PNAS reply](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC11513937/)
- An AI disclosure label simultaneously helps (novelty) and hurts (authenticity) ad attitudes. — [SAGE Open](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1177/21582440261417793)

### Inferences
- A compelling 15-minute talk could pair two papers with opposite directions (e.g., Garvey et al. "bad news → send AI" with Yalcin et al. "good news → send human"; or Zhang & Gosline "no AI aversion" with Kirk & Givi "AI-authorship penalty") and resolve them with the moderator each paper identifies (message valence, emotional vs. factual content, disclosure).
- Kovács 2024 is the single best fit for a programmer-MBA presenting to classmates: short paper, restaurant reviews, and a live "human or GPT-4?" quiz.

### Gaps
- Exact effect sizes/figures for most studies were not accessible (full texts blocked); the report writer should rely on the abstracts cited.

## Key Question 4: Which papers touch on food/beverage, café, restaurant or retail examples?

### Takeaway
Three papers are directly food/restaurant-related — Kovács 2024 (Yelp restaurant reviews), Klein & Martinez 2023 (chatbot in food e-commerce) and Longoni & Cian 2022 (food/hedonic product recommendations) — and several others use retail or consumer-product settings (Zhang & Gosline's retail product copy; Castelo et al.'s service bots; Hartmann et al.'s ad imagery; Brüns & Meißner's brand social media).

### Cited Findings
- Kovács built the stimuli from 2019 Yelp restaurant reviews and GPT-4 imitations for the same restaurants. — [Yale Insights](https://insights.som.yale.edu/insights/ai-can-write-more-believable-restaurant-review-than-human-can)
- Klein & Martinez ran their chatbot experiment in the food sector and argue food retailers lag other industries in mobile/chatbot innovation. — [RePEc abstract](https://ideas.repec.org/a/spr/elcore/v23y2023i4d10.1007_s10660-022-09562-8.html)
- Longoni & Cian's word-of-machine effect hinges on utilitarian vs. hedonic (sensory, experiential) product focus — the domain where food and beverage sit. — [AMA](https://www.ama.org/2020/11/24/when-consumers-trust-ai-recommendations-or-resist-them/)
- Zhang & Gosline's tasks included marketing copy for five retail products and a campaign to "eat less junk food." — [MIT Sloan](https://mitsloan.mit.edu/ideas-made-to-matter/study-gauges-how-people-perceive-ai-created-content)
- Castelo et al. 2023 appeared alongside HBR coverage aimed at retail/service managers. — [Open Universiteit](https://research.ou.nl/en/publications/creating-customer-service-bots-that-people-dont-hate/)

### Inferences
- A café/restaurant-themed presentation could chain Kovács (fake reviews) → Klein & Martinez (ordering chatbot) → Longoni & Cian (AI recommending a hedonic product like a dessert or specialty coffee) → Baek/Shi & Jiang (labeling AI-made menu imagery or ads).

### Gaps
- The specific food stimuli in Longoni & Cian (e.g., chocolate, recipes) could not be confirmed from accessible sources; stated from researcher memory.
- No peer-reviewed 2021–2026 paper specifically on AI-generated *product descriptions* in a top marketing journal was found; the closest are a 2025 arXiv field-experiment preprint ("Generative AI and Sales Productivity: Field Experiments in Online Retail," not peer-reviewed — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2510.12049)) and a 2024 book chapter (Peter & Lüdtke, 241 participants — [RePEc](https://ideas.repec.org/h/spr/sprchp/978-3-658-45132-5_20.html)). These are noted but excluded from the shortlist per the peer-review constraint.
